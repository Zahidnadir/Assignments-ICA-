Referee's Note: Two Averages, One Argument

O : The teaching dataset shows that prices of all four commodities increased between January 1 and March 19, 2026, in both cities.

O : Sweetener D recorded the highest percentage increase, rising by approximately 19.80% in City 1 and 19.03% in City 2.

O : Pulse C experienced the smallest increase, with prices rising by only 3.75% in City 1 and 3.64% in City 2.

O : The mean and median values are relatively close for all four commodities, and they are exactly equal for Sweetener D in both cities.

E : The small differences between the mean and median may suggest that the price observations are relatively balanced and do not contain extremely influential values.

E : The differences in percentage increases between commodities may reflect different price movements across the observation period.

C : The available dataset does not establish that inflation, government policies, transportation costs or supply shortages caused these price increases.

O : Based on the available figures, all four commodities became more expensive during the observed period, but the extent of the increase varied between commodities.

Initial Conclusion

The median and mean both provide useful information about the typical price of a commodity. However, neither average alone explains why prices changed. Therefore, both measures should be considered alongside percentage changes and the limitations of the available data.
